
# Equilibrium of Force Systems

## 1. Forces

A **force** is an external action capable of changing the motion or deforming a body.

It is a vector quantity defined by its magnitude, direction, sense, and point of application.

Forces are measured in Newtons (N) or kilonewtons (kN).

In structural engineering, forces represent loads acting on structural components, such as aerodynamic loads, weight, and inertia forces.

**Key concept:** A force tends to translate or deform a body.

## 2. Moments

A **moment** represents the rotational effect of a force about a reference point or axis.

Its magnitude depends on the force and the perpendicular distance between the reference point and the force's line of action.

\[
M = F \cdot d
\]

Where:

- \(M\) = Moment (N·m)
- \(F\) = Force (N)
- \(d\) = Perpendicular distance (m)

A moment can produce a clockwise or counterclockwise rotational tendency.

**Key concept:** A force acting at a distance from a reference point creates a moment.

## 3. Support Reactions

**Support reactions** are the forces and moments developed at structural constraints to maintain equilibrium.

Their direction and magnitude depend on the type of support and the applied loads.

Common support types in two-dimensional structural analysis include:

### Roller Support

Allows horizontal movement and rotation, while preventing vertical displacement.

- One vertical reaction.

### Pin Support

Prevents horizontal and vertical displacement, while allowing rotation.

- One horizontal reaction.
- One vertical reaction.

### Fixed Support

Prevents horizontal and vertical displacement, as well as rotation.

- One horizontal reaction.
- One vertical reaction.
- One reaction moment.

**Key concept:** Supports generate reactions that balance the applied forces and moments.
